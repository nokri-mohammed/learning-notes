**Core:** Change must be controlled, people must be helped through it, and a project must close formally. Then check that the promised benefits really arrived.

## Change control

- **Problem:** requests keep arriving mid-project, and uncontrolled additions (scope creep) hurt time, cost and quality.
- **Solution:** every change goes through a change request.

- The board, or its change authority, decides: approve, reject, defer, or raise an exception.
- Pay for approved changes from the change budget, not from the tolerance.

## People and change

- **Problem:** people resist change when they see a threat, even a good change.
- **Solution:** involve them early and communicate.

- Change curve: shock and denial, then anger and low mood, then acceptance.
- Lewin: unfreeze, change, refreeze. Kotter: eight stages, from creating urgency to making the change stick.
- Communicate early, tailor the message, and ask what people gain or lose.

## Closing

- **Problem:** without a formal end, loose ends and wasted resources continue.
- **Solution:** a formal close.

- Get formal acceptance, hand over to business as usual, release the team, keep the records.
- Record lessons learnt, and do not blame anyone: fix the problem, not the person.
- A project can end by extinction (stopped), addition (becomes a permanent unit), integration (merged into the business) or starvation (resources cut off).

## Benefits

- **Problem:** a project can finish on time and still deliver no value.
- **Solution:** a benefits realisation plan, kept after the project closes.

- For each benefit: how to measure it, a baseline before the project, an owner and a date.
- Benefits come over time, so someone must check them after close.
- Post-implementation review (PIR): done after delivery, led by someone independent. It shows whether the project met its business case and feeds lessons into future projects.

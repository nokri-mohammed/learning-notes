**Core:** A business case is the written reason to do a project: it weighs benefit, cost and risk across the options and argues for one. Without a valid business case there is no reason to start, or to carry on.

## Business case

- **Problem:** money and people are limited, so every project must justify itself.
- **Solution:** a business case that links the project to strategy and shows the benefit.

- It lists the options, the expected benefits, the cost, the risks and the financial case.
- It is the reference point for the whole project. Check it again at the end of every stage: if the case no longer holds, stop or change course.
- The Executive on the project board owns it.

## Stakeholders

- **Problem:** people who are affected by the project, or can influence it, can sink it if they are ignored.
- **Solution:** list them, sort them, and plan how to deal with each.

- Internal: sponsor, team, finance, IT. External: customers, suppliers, regulators.
- Sort by power and interest (the influence-interest grid):
  - High power, high interest: manage closely.
  - High power, low interest: keep satisfied.
  - Low power, high interest: keep informed.
  - Low power, low interest: monitor.
- Power comes from different bases (French and Raven): legitimate, reward, coercive, expert and referent.
- RACI says who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed for each task.

## Measuring value

- **Problem:** compare options when money arrives at different times.
- **Solution:** appraisal measures.

- Payback: how long until the investment is recovered.
- ROI: gain compared with cost.
- NPV (net present value): future cash flows brought back to today's value, minus the investment. Positive is good.
- IRR (internal rate of return): the discount rate at which NPV is zero.
- CapEx is spending on long-lived assets. OpEx is day-to-day running cost.

## Example

A team wants to replace an old tool. The business case compares three options: do nothing, buy a product, build it. It shows cost, benefit, risk and payback for each, then recommends one. The sponsor approves it, and the board rereads it at each stage.

**Core:** Planning answers what to make, what work that needs, how much it costs and when it finishes. Plan the near term in detail and the far term in outline.

## Breaking the work down

- **Problem:** a big project is too vague to estimate or assign.
- **Solution:** break it into parts, in three views.

- PBS (product breakdown): the things the project makes. A shopping list.
- WBS (work breakdown): the work to do. A to-do list.
- Product flow diagram: the order the products are made in.
- Gantt chart: the schedule drawn as bars on a timeline.

## Estimating and budgeting

- **Problem:** nobody knows the cost at the start, and early estimates are rough.
- **Solution:** estimate in stages, then turn the estimate into a controlled budget.

- Estimate predicts the cost. A budget allocates and controls money against it.
- Ballpark (early, rough, around plus or minus 30%), then definitive (late, accurate). Parametric uses a statistical link, such as cost per unit of work.
- Budget approaches: top-down (total first), bottom-up (sum the tasks), activity-based, time-phased.
- Contingency is money held for known risks that may or may not happen. Release it when the risk closes.
- Cost types: direct or indirect, fixed or variable.

## Scheduling

- **Problem:** tasks depend on each other, so the end date is not just the sum of durations.
- **Solution:** a network diagram, the critical path and float.

- Critical path: the longest chain of dependent tasks. If any task on it slips, the finish slips.
- Float: how much a task can slip without moving the finish. Tasks on the critical path have none.
- CPM: fixed task times, finds the longest path.
- PERT: three estimates per task (best, likely, worst), for uncertain times.
- Critical chain: adds resource limits and one buffer at the end.

## Programmes and the PSO

- A project makes one output. A programme groups related projects to deliver one strategic change.
- A Project Support Office (PSO) keeps standards, tools, lessons and records consistent across projects.

## Example

Task A (2 days) and B (3 days) both feed C (1 day). The critical path is B then C: 4 days. A has 1 day of float, because A can slip a day without delaying C.
